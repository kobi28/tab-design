Lecture date: Friday 21 March 2025 | 11:00 - 1:00pm
Instructor: Abi Bromley
Technical Checklist

Able to access lesson folder on JupyterLite/Codio/Google Slides

Exercises and Examples run as expected

Note any concerns using this form

Accessing Learner Understanding

What additional industry-based examples would help solidify learner understanding?

I will use examples from common peripheral devices learners interact with daily, such as USB keyboards, wireless mice, and Bluetooth headsets. We will also explore enterprise-grade peripherals like barcode scanners and industrial printers used in warehouse and retail settings.

What prerequisite knowledge, technical or otherwise, is needed to help learners experience success with this lesson?

Learners should have a basic understanding of how a computer motherboard communicates with external components, including familiarity with ports (USB, HDMI, Thunderbolt) and the concept of device drivers.

In what ways can you confirm that this prerequisite knowledge exists?

I will start with a quick warm-up poll asking learners to identify common port types from images. This will surface any gaps before we proceed into deeper material.
Lecture Opening

How will you introduce the lesson?

I will open by asking learners to look at their own workspace and count how many peripheral devices they are currently using. This creates an immediate personal connection to the topic and leads into a discussion about how these devices communicate with the OS.

What expectations will you set for the session?

By the end of this session, learners will be able to identify peripheral device categories, explain how plug-and-play works, troubleshoot basic driver issues, and understand the role of device managers in Windows OS.
Pacing

How long do you have to present this lesson?

2 hours (11:00am - 1:00pm) with a 10-minute break at the midpoint

If you are running low on time, what sections could you skip or shorten?

The historical overview of legacy ports (serial, parallel) can be shortened to a brief mention. The hands-on troubleshooting exercise can be condensed by doing one scenario as a group demo rather than individual practice.
Anticipating Challenges/Clarity of Explanations

Which specific concepts, examples, or exercises do you anticipate will challenge learners?

The difference between device drivers and firmware can be confusing. I will use an analogy: drivers are like translators between the device and the OS, while firmware is the device's own internal instruction set.

What questions can you anticipate from learners, and how do you plan to answer those?

"Why do some devices work without installing drivers?" — I will explain the concept of class drivers built into the OS and how USB HID devices leverage this. "What happens when a driver update fails?" — I will walk through rollback procedures in Device Manager.
Engagement

How do you plan to build rapport with the Learner?

I will share a brief personal story about a peripheral troubleshooting challenge I faced in a professional setting. Throughout the session, I will use learner names, encourage questions via the chat, and incorporate short collaborative polls every 20 minutes.

What additional resources, if any, would you share with the Learners to aid in their understanding of the concepts in this lesson?

I will share a PDF quick-reference guide for common peripheral troubleshooting steps, a link to the Microsoft Device Manager documentation, and a short video walkthrough of driver installation on Windows 11.
Checking for Understanding

What three questions would you ask as "Knowledge Checks" during the lecture to engage learners while assessing their understanding of the concepts being presented?

1. What is the primary difference between a class driver and a vendor-specific driver? 2. If a newly connected USB device is not recognised by Windows, what are the first three troubleshooting steps you would take? 3. Can you name two peripheral devices that typically require manual driver installation and explain why?
Additional Questions/Concerns
No additional concerns at this time. I have reviewed all exercise files and confirmed they load correctly in the JupyterLite environment.
